Wise can bring its funding, recurring-money, and payout experiences to the UAE on Lean's account-to-account rails, replacing card and correspondent fees with open-banking pricing. This blueprint maps each Wise flow to the Lean product that powers it, sizes the value at stake, and lays out the path to go-live.

01

Pain points & opportunities

Wise runs on local rails in its other markets - direct debit, local schemes, and card networks. Bringing the same product experience to the UAE means moving funding, recurring pulls, and payouts onto local account-to-account infrastructure. These are the remittance pain points Lean addresses, split by business and technical impact.

Business impact

Pain point / opportunityHow Lean solves itAdvantage
Card fees erode thin margins on top-ups and collections. Lean Pay by Bank for bank-to-bank top-ups and collections as a lower-cost alternative to cards. Lower take-rates, improved unit economics, and more pricing flexibility per corridor.
Volatile rates, spreads, and correspondent fees vary by corridor, squeezing competitiveness. Reduce card dependence and improve settlement predictability. Higher, more stable margins per corridor through lower acceptance costs.

Technical & operational

Pain point / opportunityHow Lean solves itAdvantage
Multiple payment methods and payout rails increase integration and ops complexity. A single integration for Pay by Bank and Payouts, with normalized references and clear statuses across banks. Faster time-to-market and leaner operations, with less engineering effort and support load.
Bank variability and inconsistent references make for a non-standard experience across banks and corridors. Consistent payment references across banks, handling different bank formats. More predictable ops and faster exception handling across markets.
Heavy AML / KYC expectations, especially source-of-funds and beneficiary ownership checks, add operational burden. Name verification on pay-ins, in development at Lean and targeted for Q4, will support payer screening and ownership checks. Reduced manual document collection, better compliance posture, smoother UX.

Told as one money lifecycle: a UAE customer funds their Wise balance, automates recurring money movement and pays out locally. The journeys split along two lines: the direction of funds (money in vs money out) and the payment permission (the customer authenticates each payment vs one standing agreement), because each combination is a different Lean product, a different customer experience, and a different piece of the build. Lean products

In scope

One-Time Payments

A single Pay by Bank payment, represented by a Charge, that funds the Wise balance directly from the customer's bank.

In scope

Recurring Payments

A Payment Agreement gives reusable permission to charge: on demand, on a schedule, or in instalments. The engine behind repeat top-ups and scheduled transfers.

Future

Payouts

Outbound transfers to UAE bank accounts and billers, initiated server-side. An open decision on Wise's side.

Future

Data

Account insights for income-based decisions, such as salary verification for lending and credit limit increases.

Future

Cards

Cards and further payment methods as values on the same Charge integration, with no re-integration.

Phase 1 · Implementation guide
One-Time Payments
Accept a single Pay by Bank payment. Each payment is a standalone Charge, tracked from creation through authentication to its outcome.
Before you start
  • Authenticate with the Lean API.
  • Create a Settlement Account for the funds you collect.
  • Configure a webhook endpoint with signature verification.
How it works
Integration checklist
  1. Create a Charge from the backend: POST /v4/payment-charges with the amount, customer, settlement_account_id and an Idempotency-Key.
  2. Send the customer to the returned hosted authentication URL: a redirect on web, or a WebView with a deep link back into the app.
  3. Treat the customer's return as the end of authentication, not the payment result.
  4. Check the outcome with GET /v4/payment-charges/{id} and handle CAPTURED, DECLINED and DISCARDED. The customer has 10 minutes to authenticate; declined and discarded are terminal, so retries mean a new Charge.
  5. Listen for charge.captured, charge.declined and charge.discarded webhooks; tolerate duplicate and out-of-order delivery.
  6. Test in Sandbox: a successful payment, a decline, abandoned authentication, an idempotent retry, and a duplicate webhook.
Endpoints
POST /v4/payment-chargesGET /v4/payment-charges/{charge_id}

Phase 1 · Implementation guide
Recurring Payments
Collect repeat payments under one Payment Agreement: reusable permission to charge the customer on demand, on a schedule, or in instalments.
Before you start
  • Authenticate with the Lean API.
  • Create a Settlement Account for the funds you collect.
  • Configure a webhook endpoint with signature verification.
How it works
Integration checklist
  1. Create a Payment Agreement: POST /v4/payment-agreements with the payment method, currency, customer and limits such as per_charge_max_amount and an expiry date.
  2. Send the customer to the hosted authentication URL to authorise the agreement.
  3. Confirm activation through the agreement.activated webhook or GET /v4/payment-agreements/{id}; only charge an ACTIVE agreement.
  4. Charge on demand with POST /v4/payment-agreements/{id}/payment-charges and an Idempotency-Key, or let Lean run a RECURRING or instalment plan on the agreement.
  5. Handle the lifecycle: pause, resume and revoke endpoints. REVOKED, FULFILLED, DECLINED and DISCARDED are terminal.
  6. Track the agreement status separately from each Charge's status, and listen for both agreement.* and charge.* webhooks.
  7. Test in Sandbox: activation, declined and abandoned authorisation, a charge over the agreement limits, revocation, and duplicate webhooks.

Phase 2 · Implementation guide
Payouts
Server-to-server outbound transfers to UAE bank accounts and billers. No customer-facing screen. Whether payouts run through Lean is an open decision on Wise's side.
Before you start
  • Authenticate with the Lean API.
  • Fund the payout account.
  • Configure payout lifecycle webhooks.
How it works
Integration checklist
  1. Create the payout destination: POST /payouts/v1/payment/destinations with the beneficiary's details.
  2. Create the payout: POST /payouts/v1/payment with an idempotency key so retries never double-pay.
  3. Treat webhooks as the source of truth for the payout lifecycle, and handle failed-payout retries explicitly.
  4. Test in Sandbox: a successful payout, a failed payout and retry, and duplicate webhooks.
